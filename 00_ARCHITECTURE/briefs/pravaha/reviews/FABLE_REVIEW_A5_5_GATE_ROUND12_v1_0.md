## The seal-approval arrangement — accuracy of §R10a-2

**Accurate.** (i) The approval is not an independent human check: dispatcher and approver are the same login and GitHub records only the login — true, and `prevent_self_review: false` is the only setting a single-account flow can live with. (ii) Claims (a)–(d) describe mechanisms that exist and that I verified at source: the generation-wide gate; the brief digest bound to a canonical payload and recomputed under the seal locks before publication; the authoritative SQL seal on the published row in the same transaction (1153 refuses a seal whose manifest is not `published`; `seal_with_approval` publishes then seals in one transaction); the deferred constraint trigger making a receipt mandatory for a first seal. (iii) The stated limit — the database enforces that a receipt exists, not that the digest is honest — is correctly stated.

**Two things §R10a-2 understates or omits.**

1. *Claim (e) is stronger than "steward discipline".* `seal_brief.brief()` refuses to produce a payload at all unless the candidate adapter is empty and every grain is VERIFIED under the pinned runner (executed: four refusal modes). The steward therefore cannot be shown an approvable brief for a non-approvable candidate; and `recompute_under_locks` repeats the same `_current_and_complete` check at seal time. The approval click is thus a *liveness/ceremony* step, not a judgment step — which is the honest way to say it.

2. *What the receipt attributes.* `approver_login` will be the owner's GitHub login for an act performed by the steward. The only field that can carry the true actor is `approved_by_note` (free text). The runbook §5.3 prescribes the comment wording; nothing in `seal_flow.py` requires the note to name the actor and the ruling. The packet should say that attribution in the receipt is to the login, not the hand, and the workflow should populate `approved_by_note` mechanically (ruling id + `github.triggering_actor`), not by hand.

**What a seal under this arrangement establishes** (given the sealing workflow is the trusted one — R12-1): at the moment of COMMIT, (1) the combined candidate gate was empty on the published row (DB-enforced by the three BEFORE triggers); (2) every included P1–P4 grain carried a current VERIFIED attestation whose `runner_identity.implementation_digest` equals the manifest's pin, written by a login that holds no write on any builder table (DB-enforced: 1240 arms + 1241 ACL closure + the job's own identity check); (3) an append-only receipt exists naming this manifest and a 64-hex digest (DB-enforced); (4) the bytes of the seven output tables, the attestations, the manifest identity and the ledger evidence at seal time hash to that digest (library-enforced — only if the workflow called `seal_with_approval`).

**What it does not establish:** that any human exercised judgment; that the verifier job's computation was independent in substance (its identity and privileges are proven, its arithmetic is trusted — §R10a says this); that `kala_gochara_coverage` and the inventory-side tables were as shown in the brief (R12-2); that the receipt's digest was ever produced by the verifier identity (R12-4).

**What must change because the approver is not independent.** Documents: the wording items above and in R12-6. Code: nothing is *required* by the ruling itself; but the ruling removes the only independent party other than the verifier identity, so the one structural compensation available — binding the receipt's digest to a brief the *verifier identity* persisted (R12-4) — moves from nice-to-have to strongly recommended. It is the owner's call; I do not make it a blocker.
