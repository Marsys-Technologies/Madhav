# Kāla — code architecture and build plan

**Written for:** the native, then the sessions that will implement each work packet and the Suvarṇa engine that will certify the result. Astra's review (REWORK) is incorporated; the reconciliation record says where and why.

## §0 · One page

**Brief.** Design the Kāla layer's *code* so that, for any chart, the data it generates delivers the most temporal value: every asset doing one thing well, the assets reinforcing one another instead of recomputing one another, and the whole rebuilding cheaply whenever L0–L2 regenerate. That is the goal. Suvarṇa certification is the receipt the layer earns along the way, not the purpose; the plan is written so certification follows from the design rather than being pursued separately (§7). Data state today is out of scope: everything upstream is being rebuilt, so everything downstream will be rebuilt.

**In plain terms, what changes for the person asking (§0.1 has the asset-by-asset version).** Today the layer can tell you which planetary period you are in, where the sky stands relative to your Moon and Lagna, which hours to avoid today, your annual context, and good days for a rite. It cannot tell you when a particular promise in your chart becomes active, whether a rough patch is coming, which of two windows is better supported, why the methods disagree, or what it predicted and what happened. After the elevation, every one of those questions is answered from one engine: a dated, cited window with the clock and the contact that produced it, what helps and what obstructs it, how much independent agreement it has against chance, how far the search looked, and a written forecast that is later scored against your life.

**The one decision.** Replace three engines that each recompute the sky, the promises and the clocks (Gochara's five generations of code at about 36,000 lines, Kṣetra at 14,000, Saṅgam at 3,000, plus 10,000 lines of writers; inventoried in §3.1: eight ephemeris/transit-scan buckets across thirteen named sites, two aspect tables that disagree on the nodes, at least eleven daśā read sites, five promise-formula families, four tārā tables, six sign-lord tables, no shared event-ontology loader, three private resume ledgers) with **one temporal engine**: a shared core library of pure primitives and readers, three evaluated stages in dependency order (judge → jury → forecaster) plus one typed negative-space producer, one candidate-then-published manifest, and every other output a *read model* over those stages. Not every duplicate disappears by moving into a library — independent verifiers, continuous knots and method-specific conventions are kept on purpose (§3.1). No asset id is created or deleted; eleven existing writers become read-model writers; four become stage writers; the rest stay as adapters or services.

**Why this is worth it, in three measures.**
- *Value.* Every window the native sees will carry the same six things: the clock, the contact, the structure engaged, the enabling and inhibiting conditions, the coverage of the search, and the typed null where something could not be derived. Agreement between methods becomes a measured quantity with a null, not a count. Negative space has six honest states. Forecast probability appears only under a declared event model. None of this is possible while three engines store three shapes.
- *Structure.* One object model (assertion envelope with typed payloads · issued_forecast · outcome) and one evidence algebra (roles, roots, derivation parents, selection ancestry) make duplicate counting impossible by type, not by review. A read model is SQL over stage tables where the stage rows carry its inputs; where they do not, the missing input is added to a stage rather than computed inside the reader (§4.3).
- *Build.* Geometry, interpretation and evaluation are three lineages across the whole layer. The orchestrator already skips a writer whose receipt-visible inputs are unchanged and propagates `output_changed` along `depends_on`; a stage reuses its own geometry when its recorded input vector says it can (§6.2 item 3). Dense materialisation (8.57 million field rows) may be replaced by a compact evaluator **once it passes the numerical contract in §6.2** — not before. Chart preparation happens once per candidate and is content-hashed; every substep binds to that hash.

**What this plan leaves untouched.** The judge (Gochara 5.0) is Pravāha's and its spec is FROZEN; this plan *consumes* it and proposes exactly three amendments through the steward (§12). Kṣetra's ten rulings and B8-6 stand. N-32 stands. D-SCOPE stands. The FROZEN orchestrator contract is not extended; every stage is a `WriterBase` writer on `ctx.db_conn`.

**Every asset's algorithm is elevated, not only its plumbing.** The companion KALA_ASSET_ALGORITHM_ELEVATIONS_v1_1.md gives each active asset a card at the Gochara depth: the algorithm as the code computes it today (constants quoted), its defects, the elevated algorithm as typed inputs → computation → outputs → nulls, the classical basis with each citation labelled by where it was verified (served corpus, local OCR, prior document), what it gives to and takes from the other assets, and the oracle that would fail if it were wrong. Fourteen algorithm-level findings came out of that pass (its §4), among them that the field's clock term is identically zero in the current implementation and that the muhūrta cancellation doctrine the election view says is missing is in the served corpus and was never extracted. The packets in §9 implement those cards; §5 of the companion maps card to packet.

**The three family plans are folded in, item by item.** §8.1 maps every ruled or decided item of the Gochara-family plan (v2.2 and the 2026-09-24 rulings), the Kṣetra ecosystem plan (v1.17, rulings 1–10, PCD-1/2, B8-*) and the Saṅgam packet (R-1…R-6, E1…E6, the concept note v1.1) to the module and work packet that now carries it, or says which later ruling superseded it. Nothing ratified is dropped silently.

**What must be ruled before code starts** (§12; reconciliation §7). First, the four semantic corrections Astra's review made and this revision adopts: distinct daśā constructions (never aliased), effective cancellation states (a cancelled obstruction is not operationally active), path-specific rather than universal period gating, and separate field and null estimands. Second, two contracts: candidate → verification → publication (§4.4) and forecast issuance (episode key, immutable issue key, delivery linkage; table location R-11). Third, the three plumbing rulings carried from v1.0: the core library may import the judge's pure modules (R-1); the first slice uses the judge's own sky tables and needs **no** new registry id (R-2, as recommended); the views move into the retrieval registry (R-4). Fourth, the revised small value ablation runs before any forecaster spend (R-12). The first packet to code is a working vertical slice (K0a), not an importable skeleton.

## §1 · Design principles (binding on every packet)

1. **Compute each temporal primitive once; store the smallest evaluable representation that passes its numerical contract; serve by manifest.** A primitive is: a sky boundary event, a contact, a period boundary, a mechanism edge, a rule evaluation, a segment support vector, a field coefficient. If two stages need it, it lives in the core library and in one table — unless it is an independent verifier's restatement, a continuous knot source (B8-6) or a method-specific convention, which are kept on purpose (§3.1).
2. **Three lineages, layer-wide** (Gochara spec §0, generalised): geometry · interpretation · evaluation. A change re-runs the lineage it alters and everything downstream, through the orchestrator's receipt-visible inputs and `depends_on` edges (§6.2 item 3). The dependency decides, never the label on the change.
3. **One envelope, typed payloads.** Every stage writes `assertion` rows sharing one envelope (identity, subject, interval, roots, provenance, coverage, null reason) with a **typed payload per stage**; forecasts are `issued_forecast`; outcomes are `outcome`. One universal row schema is not attempted — the present frame, provenance and grain enums cannot express every annual, empirical and intraday output.
4. **The evidence algebra is a type, not a convention.** `role ∈ {selects, conditions, qualifies, corroborates, explains}`, `roots`, `derivation_parents`, `used_for_selection`, `operator_role ∈ {scored, testimony}`. A reused root yields no increment; a root used for selection never corroborates; testimony weighs nothing; enforced by the library and mutation tests.
5. **Typed nulls; declared constants.** One `null_reason` vocabulary for the layer. A constant is permitted when its meaning, provenance and use are declared beside it; an **undeclared** literal in a stage module fails lint. ("No default anywhere" is not the rule — legitimate constants exist; undeclared ones do not.)
6. **Candidate-scoped everything.** Every row carries `generation`; a run builds a **candidate** generation pinned at its start; nothing is shared across generations; publication is a separate operation after completeness and verification (§4.4); replace-then-insert is scoped `(chart_id × generation × grain)` inside the substep that owns the grain (§N.3); immutable records (issued forecasts, published generations) are never deleted to satisfy a detector.
7. **Read models are SQL over stage tables where the stage rows carry their inputs.** A reader that needs a distinction the stage rows do not carry (§13 Q2 lists them) gets that input added to a stage; it does not compute it locally and it does not lose it. "Pure SQL" is a consequence of adequate inputs, not an acceptance criterion.
8. **Verification: the builder self-checks and reports; a separately dispatched verification job, by an independent implementation under the verifier principal, writes the verification rows** (the judge's actual separation, R9-6.1). A builder-written verification row is not verification. A stage without a dispatched verifier has no `PASS`.
9. **Frozen contracts are consumed, not extended.** `WriterBase` (`run` / `plan_substeps` + `run_substep`, `ctx.db_conn` never committed), the Gochara spec v1.4 **with its delegated rulings of 2026-10-02**, the Kṣetra rulings (B8-6 verbatim), N-32. If a packet needs more, it stops and routes (§12).
10. **Certification is measured, not declared.** Every gate in §7 names the actual detector and the verdict it can read today (PASS / measured N/A / PARTIAL / NO_DETECTOR); an asset passes because of how it is built and because the detector measured it, never because a reviewer looked or a pack was approved.

### 4.3 Read-model writer template

```python
@register("ka_kala_darshana")
class KalaDarshanaWriter(WriterBase):
    def run(self, ctx):
        gen = manifest.candidate_generation(ctx)                     # the candidate this build_id opened; never "current"
        rows = ctx.db_conn.execute(DARSHANA_SQL, gen)                # SQL over judge / jury / negative-space tables of that candidate
        return idempotency.replace_candidate_partition(
            ctx.db_conn, "kala_darshana", ctx.config["chart_id"], gen, grain_key=None, rows=rows)
```

The SQL is reviewed as the read model's whole semantics; it may join, filter and aggregate, and it may not add a rule (§1 principle 7). `count_sql` counts `(chart_id, generation)` with a depth-0 `chart_id = $1`. `integrity_check_sql` is written for the engine as it runs it: unparameterised, truthy first column means pass, scoped to the chart by reading the running `build_runs` row **and valid after the run completes** (a query that only resolves while a build is running is not an integrity check). Ldgr's source-presence detector is satisfied because every output row carries the assertion ids it joined (it does not resolve them — §7); Dens because the read model carries the stage's tier column through and the serving capability declares its contract on the same entry.

### 4.4 Candidate → verification → publication (closes the cycle Astra found)

v1.0's template read the *published* generation while the layer manifest was to publish a *complete* generation including its read models — a cycle. The sequence is now:

1. **Open candidate.** The first chart-domain substep of the run opens a candidate generation bound to `build_id` (`state = building`), pinning natal tier, conventions, F1/F2 builds, rule registry version. Every later substep and read model in the run resolves `candidate_generation(ctx)` from that binding. No reader resolves "current" by itself.
2. **Build.** Stages and read models write into the candidate's partitions. A substep that finds its recorded input vector unchanged may reuse its own grain (judge rule §10.1); the orchestrator's delta-skip handles the whole-asset case (§6.2 item 3).
3. **Completeness attestation.** The candidate row records every included grain's result (zero rows is a result) — the judge's "every included grain is verified" rule, layer-wide.
4. **Verification.** A separately dispatched job under the verifier principal re-derives by independent implementation and writes verification rows (§1 principle 8).
5. **Publish.** `publish_head(candidate)` compare-and-swaps the published head only when 3 and 4 pass; the previous head is retained (reachability), never deleted. Serving reads the head; nothing serves a candidate.
6. **Rollback** = compare-and-swap back to the retained head; rehearsed in K9.

Issued forecasts are outside this cycle: they are written on delivery against a *published* head's cutoff and are never part of a candidate's replaceable partitions.

## §5 · The serving plane

Finding F-L19 of the review is structural, stated by reach layer: **registry reach** exists for several L3 capabilities (e.g. `query_activation_waveform.ts` reads `kala_taranga`; `kala_windows_get` resolves); **MCP-facade reach** exists for the eight views in the MCP package; **web-floor reach** does not exist for the named views — the generated Vidhi bridge counts 29 unmapped live-tool entries and the arrival line is a placeholder. The views' TypeScript also re-implements calendar logic the core library owns.

**Change.** Each view becomes **one retrieval-registry composite capability** (the pattern `judgment_query` already uses in `register_d9_judgment.ts`), with: `density_contract` declared, `empty_reason` discipline, drill pointers, and the manifest id on every response. The MCP facades in `platform-mcp/src/tools/kala_views/` become thin aliases of the registry capability (as `register_p1_aliases.ts` already does for several tools). The Vidhi bridge then resolves `now_read`, `ahead_read`, `priority_read`, `elect_read`, `story_read`, `ritual_read`, `explain_read` by construction, because the capability's own name is the live tool name (`catalog_name_direct`).

**Rules.** A view reads stage tables and the manifest only; it never computes a primitive (the calendar primitives are read from the table F2/calendar wrote, or called through the service facade); confirmed, testimony and catalog-only rows are counted separately (§N.6); the `'3.0'`-era rows are labelled `context_only` until retired; `kala_taranga` gets a facade or is dropped from the Vidhi primitive list (today the primitive points at a tool that does not read it).

### 6.2 The efficiency design

1. **Sky once, chart-independent.** Boundary events per body per convention are geocentric and shared across every chart (Gochara spec §6.1). Build once; a new chart solves only its contacts against stored boundaries. Moon and day tier on demand with a coverage record, never materialised. Sharing a Python package does not share in-memory context across orchestrator invocations; sharing happens through tables.
2. **Chart preparation once per candidate.** A `ChartContext` (natal facts at pinned tier, conventions, F1 graph, F2 boundaries) is built in the first substep, serialised and content-hashed as the candidate's `input_vector`; every later substep binds to the hash and refuses on mismatch. No per-class re-preparation (strategy P2).
3. **Invalidation as the runner actually provides it** [S `asset_runner.py:279–403, 1121, 1138–1154, 1390–1416`; `staleness.py:21–45, 93–101`]. The frozen runner (a) digests the writer's source **including its local import closure**, (b) **skips a data writer whose receipt-visible inputs (code digest, chart config, upstream digest, partition declaration) match the previous receipt**, (c) records whether the output content changed, and (d) propagates `output_changed` through transitive `depends_on` edges. So: a truthful `depends_on` gives **whole-asset** invalidation and content-based downstream propagation; **grain-level reuse is the writer's own decision** from its recorded input vector (the judge's §10.1 rule: re-solve geometry or only re-score, and record which fired). What this does **not** give: an L2 attachment-only change and an L2 change that alters an admitted interpretation are both "F1 changed" to the runner — the distinction is tested separately (K9 lineage drill) and claimed only to the extent the drill shows. A manifest-only change invisible to code/config/upstream receipts would be skipped before the writer runs; so conventions and rule versions are made receipt-visible (part of config or an upstream digest), never manifest-only. No finer scheduling is attributed to truthful edges than this.
4. **Compact field only under a numerical contract.** The breakpoint set is operator-complete for the model actually in force: envelope rise/plateau/fall knots and exact contact extrema; roots where the selected maximum envelope changes; crossings of `min(Moon, Lagna)` and other piecewise operators; applicable clock boundaries and scenario boundaries; obstruction/cancellation and risk-mask edges; horizon boundaries and circular-shift seams; **and adaptive refinement for the curved `ln(1 − ρu)` term** (W2 §5.2: τ = 0.02 nats, depth ≤ 6 — the stored representation is bounded, not exact). Acceptance of any compact evaluator against the corrected reference: structural equality of segment partitions and coefficients where the same representation is claimed; a per-segment analytic or bounded-error justification; one-sided evaluation at every discontinuity; adversarial interior switching/peak fixtures; random differential evaluations and interval integrals as supplementary; **reproduction of the null statistics**, not only the unshifted field. Bitwise equality for unchanged deterministic operations; a declared tolerance where evaluation order changes; neither preserves the known zero-clock bug. Dense rows are untouched until this passes. Expected segment counts are measured, not promised.
5. **Shared null preparation, separate estimands.** The shift schedule, segment preparation and draw machinery live in `kala_core.measure` and run once per candidate. On that shared preparation, **three experiments** with their own estimand and conditioning: the jury's incremental-agreement null for `D(W)`, the whole-pipeline selection null, and the forecaster's class-specific field-maximum null (duration-bucket sliding maxima, pooled 0.95 quantile). Denominator = non-identity shifts + the observation (today 1,023 + 1 = 1,024; W2's text says R = 256 and the code 1,024 — the code's value is pinned and declared, not mechanically changed). Circular shifts are not assumed exchangeable; the surrogate flag is carried [M Phipson–Smyth 2010].
6. **Read models rebuild cheaply — measured, not promised.** Eleven writers that today compute become SQL; their cost is a join over the candidate's partitions. The figure is measured in K9 under §6.3; no "seconds" is claimed before then.
7. **Substep grains that resume.** Heavy stages plan per `class`, each a replace-then-insert of its own candidate partition inside one orchestrator transaction. **The 600 s timeout is a bound to measure against, not a property of "one class per substep"**: expensive null work may need replicate blocks or other resumable grains, decided from the K9 measurements. Coverage partitions are written first (pin 7). Resume is content-bound (candidate + input vector + code + grain) through `kala_core.resume`, never positional, and **`plan_substeps` never deletes**.
8. **The DHARA null's cost.** The 68 % attributed to the shift null is credible from the source (repeated preparation, dense per-row work). The strategy's P1 names **two** operations — an exact numeric slice maximum (the sliding-window maximum per duration bucket) **and** an exact blocked order-statistic reducer (the pooled quantile) — they address different reductions and neither replaces the other. Replicate count is a declared parameter with a power argument. The saving is a hypothesis until measured on the same corrected model and workload.
9. **No learned weights, no 25-parameter fit, no composite salience at first build** (concept §9 cut list). The first generation is G1 (algorithm document 3.20): structurally repaired and cheap; enrichment is admitted step by step with its own oracle and cost class.

### 6.3 Benchmark contract (strategy §5, adopted; measures per Astra D3)

Before any speed claim: exact source/dependency/model versions, hardware, ephemeris files, workload dimensions, cache state. Measured separately: total wall and CPU time and peak memory; context preparation, astronomy, field evaluation, null maxima and quantile; SQL round trips, bytes, rows and WAL; cold, warm, resume-after-kill and restart loss; no-window, dense-window, station/retrograde and boundary workloads; time to the first complete qualified consumer result. **A semantic change and an optimisation are never combined into one "faster and equivalent" claim** — the corrected model is benchmarked first, then the optimisation against it. Rehearsal database first; the canonical chart only through the orchestrator under the campaign's standing authority, with a backup before every production rebuild and a dry run before any irreversible delete (N-154). **No minutes-per-chart figure is promised by this plan.**

### 6.4 The full-layer rebuild (what Suvarṇa's layer close requires)

One orchestrator run, `action=rebuild, clear_before=false`, over the L3 asset list in level order, canonical chart. With this architecture the list is: candidate open → sky (shared, skipped by the runner's delta-skip if its convention is unchanged) → F2 self-test, `ka_yojaka` → `ka_avadhi` → `ka_gochara_v5` → `ka_vighnakara` → `ka_sangam` → `ka_kshetra` → the four read models → service self-tests → verification job → publish. ## §7 · Certification as measured — the nine gates against the inspected detectors

Suvarṇa certifies an asset when every core gate reads `PASS` or a rule-computed `N/A`, every declared addition is certified, no open gap row remains, and a disposition is recorded; "current" means the certificate still matches the writer's file hashes, upstream certificate ids, the declarations hash, the registry revision and the asset's semantic fingerprint [S `nikasha_certify.py:1020–1046, 1131–1145`; `asset_census.py` CRITERION_REGISTRY at revision 25, lines 169–215]. Verdicts roll up worst-first; `nikasha_certify.py` **refuses an invented verdict** (`census_mismatch`), **requires `NO_DETECTOR` where the registry detector is `NONE`** (`detector_none`), caps `Null.*` and `Narr.fidelity_test` at PARTIAL unless earned, and requires the semantic fingerprint and full writer-file set for any PASS or measured N/A.

**Governance, kept to the load-bearing minimum.** Four rules: (1) every writer is a frozen-contract `WriterBase` writer whose writes name their own tables statically; (2) a backup precedes every production rebuild and a dry run precedes any irreversible delete, canonical chart only, through PRs to `main` (N-154); (3) served output is never wrong or partial without an authority switch or a disclosure (N-29) — the candidate→publish compare-and-swap provides it; (4) each **implemented** asset ships a small, version-specific certification mapping (below) with its packet — not a universal declarations generator before one real asset proves the schema. Everything else in the campaign's governance is consumed as given.

## §11 · Risks

1. **Import direction into a frozen kernel.** If the steward refuses R-1, `kala_core` duplicates the kernel's pure modules for one generation, with a byte-equality test between the two; the design does not change.
2. **Scope of "new assets" under Suvarṇa.** If a shared-domain sky row is out of scope, the judge's own sky tables remain the substrate and the other stages read them (R-2 fallback); cost: the sky is rebuilt with the judge's chart generation rather than once per convention.
3. **Strangler columns on live tables.** Re-shaping `kala_activation_predicates`, `kala_convergence`, `kala_obstruction`, `kala_field` in place touches L4 cascades (`phala_anchors`) and L5 binds; every re-shape is additive first, consumers migrate, old columns retire — with the N-32 wave order and transitive-footprint statement per wave.
4. **B8-6 (Kṣetra's own knots).** The forecaster keeps its continuous kinematics for evaluation until a shared-geometry equivalence is demonstrated; two solvers coexist for one generation by ruling, not by accident.
5. **Insufficient n.** Most discriminative tests will read `insufficient_evidence` for a long time; the product must say so (concept §10). The code is designed so that reads as a typed state, not a failure.
6. **One build slot.** Stage-level parallelism is not available; efficiency comes from not recomputing, not from concurrency. If the orchestrator's single-slot law changes, the grains are already independent.
7. **Coordination debt.** F1 as the judge's natal-fact source, G-E3/G-E4, the N-32 storage addition, W6 withdrawal and corpus counts close elsewhere (concept §8.5); each is a dependency of a packet above and is listed in it.
8. **Live hazards the refactor must not carry across** (code trace): Saṅgam's destructive `plan_substeps`; the legacy `brahmagyan/kala` CLI writing `kala_convergence`/`kala_obstruction`; the two `SPECIAL_DRISHTI_DEG` tables disagreeing on the nodes; the two `orb_strength_score` formulas; the century writer writing both staging and production tables; ledger fingerprints that include `date.today()`. Each gets a regression oracle in K0 so it cannot reappear.
9. **Geometry model choice.** Two numerically different models coexist: the step scanners (`transit_search`, v1/v3) and the arc-index + Swiss-refined solver (kernel, w2g, Kṣetra S0). The plan shares geometry *infrastructure* (ephemeris access, conventions, caches, bracketing, identities) and keeps separately specified solvers for contacts, sign boundaries, annual returns and composite extrema; interior extrema of λ-valued forms need their own proof, never a return to scanning.
10. **Publication cycle regression.** Any reader that resolves "current" on its own re-opens the cycle §4.4 closes; the lint "no reader resolves a generation except through `candidate_generation(ctx)` or the served head" is part of K0a.
11. **Strangler-column failure modes on the four re-shaped tables.** `kala_activation_predicates`: old consumers expect predicate/target rows, a graph has nodes, edges and attachments — one old row key cannot silently become all three; `kala_convergence`: `peak_date IS NOT NULL` filters can erase correct jury rows that have no peak; `kala_obstruction`: numeric overrides and typed states coexisting can both be applied; `kala_field`: segment coefficients, time origin, units, class/risk identity and weight versions must stay explicit. Mitigation: versioned compatibility views or explicit reader versions, generation-bound keys, consumer-contract fixtures (missing peak, empty result, unavailable source, disconnected interval, duplicate attachment, preserved outcome references). N-32 stays in force.
12. **G2 remains unspecified.** Until K5's brief supplies the replacement model's elements (algorithm 3.20), the forecaster is G1; the plan's value claims for the forecaster are those of a repaired G1 and the ablation's verdict, nothing more.
