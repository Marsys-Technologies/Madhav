## 1. Verdict and the five findings that matter most

**The chosen direction stands: Gochara as judge, Saṅgam as jury, Kṣetra as the odds and attention stage. The proposed implementation is not ready to build.** It finds substantial defects in the existing layer, but several replacements remain descriptions of desirable outputs rather than algorithms capable of producing them. The greatest risk is spending the rebuild budget on a cleaner architecture that silently changes the astrology, preserves an undefined field model, and still cannot publish or certify its results.

**First, the forecaster’s replacement mathematics is missing.** Removing noisy-OR promise strength, arbitrary conductance and suppression multipliers may be justified. But a signed graph and six qualitative states do not themselves define a positive event intensity. The plan still promises an integral, likelihood, null distribution and monthly waveform without specifying their replacement function. Its proposed breakpoints also omit changes that the existing function needs. This must be settled before compact-field engineering begins. [J][C]

**Second, the publication sequence contains a cycle.** The projection template refuses to read an unpublished generation, while the layer manifest is supposed to publish a complete generation including its projections. The plan needs a pinned candidate that builders can read, a completed verification step, and a separate publication operation. Its universal delete-and-insert helper also conflicts with immutable issued forecasts and retained published generations. These are correctness requirements, not additional governance. [J][C]

**Third, several “projections” introduce new astrological judgments.** Kalasutra adds a universal participant-lord period intersection to already evaluated judge windows. That can narrow a window under a rule the judge never applied, including through testimony-only subdivisions. Taranga promises domain integrals without defining how heterogeneous event-class rates become a domain field. Jivana Parva’s proposed oracle erases differences in conditions when mechanism identities stay the same. SQL does not make these transformations doctrinally neutral. [J]

**Fourth, the clock card conflates distinct methods.** Its Cara construction imports a passage about kāraka daśā; its alternate-start scenarios import Sārāvalī’s Mūla-daśā rule into the Moon-star ladder; its competence partition restricts Kālacakra to health/life-force although the local BPHS text expressly discusses wealth, education and other results. These are not harmless labels. They would determine which evidence the layer can express. [D][J]

**Fifth, cancellation and negative space need an effective-state algebra, not a signed flag.** A cancelled adverse formation is not necessarily negative evidence; an obstruction carrying `cancelled_by` must not remain operationally active; unavailable Sarvatobhadra data does not make the method inapplicable. The proposed negative-space producer also consumes `measured_lower_rate` from the forecaster that depends on it, creating another cycle. Preserve the underlying findings and their contests, but separately compute what is effective, for which result, during which interval. [J]

One correction to my intermediate assessment matters: **the later delegated Gochara ruling expressly excludes viparīta cancellation.** I do not recommend reversing that ruling. Card 3.12 follows its direction; card 3.4 and the unamended v1.4 language do not consistently reflect it. The applicable record is `NR-VIPAREETA-20261002`, not the older specification alone.

### B4. Is the six-state negative-space typology complete?

**[J][D] No. It is a useful user-facing vocabulary, but not a sufficient internal model.**

### C4. Compact knots, equality and shared null

**[C][M][J] The proposed knot set is insufficient.**

For the current model, retain at least:

- Envelope rise/plateau/fall knots and exact contact extrema.
- Roots where the selected maximum envelope changes.
- Crossings in `min(Moon,Lagna)` and other piecewise operators.
- Applicable clock boundaries and uncertainty/scenario boundaries.
- Obstruction/cancellation and risk-mask edges.
- Horizon boundaries and circular-shift seams.
- Adaptive refinement needed for curved log-suppression terms.

W2 explicitly defines the stored log-linear representation separately from the underlying product function (`W2:633–650`). “Exact integration” is exact for that representation. It is not proof that the representation exactly equals every underlying factor.

**No finite set of sampled instants proves byte equality of arbitrary functions.** A useful acceptance contract combines:

1. Structural equality of segment partitions and coefficients where the same representation is claimed.
2. Analytic or bounded-error justification on each segment.
3. One-sided evaluation at all discontinuities.
4. Adversarial interior switching/peak fixtures.
5. Random differential evaluations and interval integrals as supplementary tests.
6. Reproduction of null statistics, not only the unshifted field.

Bitwise equality is appropriate for unchanged deterministic operations; a changed evaluation order may require a declared numerical tolerance. Neither tolerance nor equality should preserve the known zero-clock bug.

**One null per generation is not the same estimand.** Shared preparation and a shared shift schedule can save work. Jury incremental agreement, whole-pipeline selection and class-specific field maxima still need separate statistics and conditioning.

Also, the current DHARA engine already uses **1,023 nonidentity shifts plus the observation**, giving denominator 1,024 (`dhara_null.py:166–175`). Do not mechanically change it to 1,025. Conversely, deterministic circular shifts are not automatically independent or exchangeable. The finite-sample correction does not validate the chosen null hypothesis. [Phipson and Smyth, 2010](https://gksmyth.github.io/pubs/PermPValuesPreprint.pdf).
