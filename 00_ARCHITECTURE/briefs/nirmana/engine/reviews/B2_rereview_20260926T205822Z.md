## The semantic question — settled, with the evidence

**The behaviour is SEVERANCE, and `plan.ts` severs identically.**

Filtering `edges` on the **dependent** side drops the edge `(I, A)` when `I` is inactive, while
keeping `(B, I)` because `B` is active. The recursion extends a closure pair only through
`e.dependent = c.upstream`, so `B`'s chain terminates at `I` and never reaches `A`. I did not infer
this — I built it. A five-node registry `A ← I(inactive) ← B` plus a control chain `A ← M ← C`,
loaded into a throwaway Postgres, with **the real migration applied verbatim**:

| | A | B | C | M |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **the shipped view** | **2** | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| **the real `computeDownstreamClosure()` from `plan.ts`**, given the `is_active` population `runs/route.ts` actually loads | **2** (`{C, M}`) | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| *(control)* `plan.ts` given all rows including inactive — **not** what production feeds it | 4 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

**They agree, and they agree by construction, not by coincidence.** The mechanisms are isomorphic:
the view drops inactive rows from the edge source, `transitiveDownstream()` never iterates them
because they are absent from its registry array. An inactive asset cannot be a bridge in either. The
implementer's stated reasoning is exactly right, and the migration header states it explicitly — *"so
an inactive asset can never seed further reachability either"*.

**Is the semantics defensible?** Yes, and it is the right one for this figure. The view's own purpose
is "how many assets would need a **rebuild**". `B` sits behind an asset nothing will ever build, so
`B` is already unbuildable for reasons that have nothing to do with `A`. Counting it in `A`'s number
would tell an operator that rebuilding `A` unblocks something it cannot unblock. Severance also keeps
the view answering the same question as the build planner, which is the entire point of Decision 4.
**Sustained.**

**Does such a path exist in the real registry today? No — and that matters.** Read-only against
production: `SELECT … WHERE a.is_active AND NOT i.is_active` returns **zero rows**, and the count of
*any* asset depending on *either* inactive asset is **0**. Both inactive assets
(`ka_gochara_v3_century_materialize`, `ka_gochara_sweep`) are pure sinks. So the 127-asset real-data
comparison — the packet's headline proof — **never exercises the severance case at all**. Nor does
the synthetic fixture: its `inactive_dependent` is also a pure sink.

**Which makes the suite blind to the semantics it was written to defend.** I constructed the
non-severing variant — leave `edges` unfiltered and exclude inactive assets only at the outer join,
so a chain *through* an inactive asset is **not** cut — and ran the corrected suite against it:

- on the `A ← I ← B` probe it gives **A = 3**, not 2. A materially different view.
- against the corrected suite: **11 passed (11)**. Not one assertion can tell the two apart.

So the guarantee is real; the suite's *evidence* for it is vacuous on this axis. The coordinator's
framing was right to press here, and the honest record is the precise one: **agreement by
construction — verified by this reviewer on a constructed case — with no detector of its own.**
