## §2 — The rollback: **the load-bearing unknown, and it is load-bearing in the implementer's favour**

The implementer declared this "defensive and untested against real psycopg failed-transaction
semantics". That caveat is honest and it is also **over-modest**. I tested it on a **real psycopg
connection to the read-only production proxy** — no write was attempted or made:

```
A. fresh connection            : IDLE
B. after a normal statement    : INTRANS
C. fault raised                : UndefinedTable
D. transaction status now      : INERROR
E. statement WITHOUT rollback  : FAILED -> InFailedSqlTransaction --
                                 "current transaction is aborted, commands ignored until end of transaction"
F. after conn.rollback()       : IDLE
G. statement AFTER rollback    : SUCCEEDED -> {'n': 0}
   (real parameterised SELECT against the real build_run_assets table)
H. UPDATE on read-only conn    : refused as expected -> ReadOnlySqlTransaction
I. closed cleanly; nothing committed
```

**Line E is the finding.** Without the rollback, the terminalize would have hit
`InFailedSqlTransaction` and written nothing — F-1 would have been fixed **only against `FakeConn`**,
which is precisely the hazard the gate asked me to attack. The rollback is not belt-and-braces; it is
the thing that makes the fix real. Line H confirms my probe was genuinely read-only.

**What remains untested, stated plainly:** G proves the connection is usable again and can issue a
real statement with real parameters against the real table. It does **not** prove the two *writes*
commit, because the proxy refuses writes (H). That residue is small and named: it is the ordinary
`_mark_asset_error_terminal` path, which the BLOCKED cascade already executes in production for
1,281 of the campaign's 2,283 failure records. **What it would take to close it fully:** one
divergence-plus-fault exercised against a writable non-production database with the same schema, or
a post-deploy observation. I would not hold the commit for it.

**The degenerate case is handled honestly.** If the fault is a dead connection, `conn.rollback()`
itself raises (swallowed by the inner `except`), and `_terminalize_diverged_assets` raises too —
caught, and logged as *"failed to terminalize diverged asset(s) … the divergence record is lost for
this attempt"*, with the original exception re-raised unchanged. That is §N.8 done right: the code
states the loss rather than presenting a clean result it did not earn.

## §5 — G-2 (NEW): a severity correction to **my own** prior F-1 framing

At the last gate I wrote that on the hard-fault path the divergence was "discarded", "lost", "never
written". **I did not check what happens afterwards, and I should have.** Having now read the
watchdog:

- `platform/src/app/api/cockpit/watchdog/route.ts` **sweep 3** terminalizes
  `state='planned' AND started_at IS NULL AND created_at < NOW() - INTERVAL '10 minutes'` — which is
  exactly the shape a pre-claim hard fault leaves behind.
- The **orphan reaper** terminalizes `state='running'` after 30 minutes with 15 minutes of no
  heartbeat — exactly the shape G-1's orphan-cleanup fault leaves behind.
- Both stamp the run's still-`queued` rows `state='aborted'` with a **generic** message.

So the honest claim is **not** "the record is lost forever". It is: **the divergence *reason* is lost,
and the row is eventually terminalized with a misattributing message.** That is a smaller claim than I
made — and, read properly, a sharper one: `UNDISPATCHED_RUN_MESSAGE` ("orphan-watchdog: run never
dispatched") is **Family B's signature**, the "run dispatched with no manifest at all" bucket that
A3's own before-measurement went to considerable lengths to separate from Family A. A registry
divergence silently re-labelled as a dispatch failure is precisely the population-blending the packet
exists to undo.

**The coordinator should carry G-2 into the report in place of my earlier wording.** F-1 was still
worth fixing, and is fixed; but "lost and misattributed into the wrong causal family after 10–45
minutes" is the claim the evidence supports, and "lost forever" is not.

**Recommended report wording:** F-1 and F-5 are opposing-direction errors *and* F-1 slightly widened
F-5's write surface in the course of narrowing its own loss. That is the complete picture; "disjoint,
therefore unchanged" is half of it.
