# Owner Rulings — PARISESA-V4, 2026-08-21

## R-1 — F-23, Question (i): Saturn bīja — *śanaiścarāya* or *śanaye*?

**RULING: The Devanagari is correct; the transliteration field is wrong. Fix the
transliteration on `saturn_matrix_mantra` to `oṃ prāṃ prīṃ prauṃ saḥ śanaiścarāya
namaḥ` (IAST).**

Basis, in descending order of strength:

1. **Internal consistency (decisive on its own, no doctrinal judgment needed).** The
   two fields on one row must transliterate the same string. The Devanagari reads
   शनैश्चराय (*śanaiścarāya*); the transliteration says *Shanaye*. One of them is
   wrong about *what the row says*, before any question of what it *should* say. The
   row's own sibling (`sat_career_mantra_01`) and the Devanagari agree against the
   transliteration, 2-to-1 within the corpus itself.
2. **The tradition (confirming, not load-bearing).** In the Navagraha bīja formula
   the completion after `prāṃ prīṃ prauṃ saḥ` is standardly *śanaiścarāya namaḥ* —
   Śanaiścara ("the slow mover") being the formal name used in the bīja and stotra
   tradition. *Oṃ śaṃ śanaye namaḥ* is a real and widely used mantra, but it is the
   **short nāma-mantra**, a different formula — not the completion of the bīja string
   this row stores. The transliteration error looks like contamination from that
   shorter form.

Confidence: HIGH — and note the ruling is safe even for a reader who distrusts my
Sanskrit entirely, because ground (1) alone decides it.

## R-2 — F-23, Question (ii): Does BPHS attest the bīja set?

**RULING: Treat the `BPHS Ch.91–94` attribution for the 9 bīja-matrix rows as
incorrect-as-stated. Reattribute to the tantric/compiled bīja tradition
(`Navagraha bīja tradition; compiled in Mantra Mahodadhi (Mahīdhara, 16th c.)` or
equivalent), retaining a BPHS reference only as `upāya context`, clearly labeled.**

Basis: BPHS's graha-śānti material prescribes the **Vedic ṛks**, donations, and
propitiation procedures per graha; the `prāṃ prīṃ prauṃ`-class bīja mantras belong to
the tantric/āgamic layer transmitted through compilations like Mantra Mahodadhi and
the Navagraha stotra literature, not to BPHS itself. The investigating agent's own
provisional suspicion ("could not verify that BPHS actually contains the bīja set")
matches this. Under the product's own B.3 discipline, a citation that cannot be
located in the cited text must not stand; reattribution to the tradition that actually
transmits the material is both more honest and more defensible.

Confidence: HIGH on "do not leave the BPHS attribution standing unverified"; MEDIUM-HIGH
on the positive Mantra Mahodadhi attribution — if Lane 4 later quotes attestation text,
quote it from the edition actually consulted, whatever that turns out to be.

## R-3 — F-23, Question (iii): Nakshatra rows — TB ṛks or nāma-mantras?

**RULING: The nāma-mantra form (`oṃ mitrāya namaḥ` etc.) IS the intended product and
stays as the primary served/japa form. The Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 3.1.1–2 ṛks are Lane-4
enrichment (as `classical_attestation_text` + an added citation), not a replacement.
Correct every `classical_ref` on these 27 rows now to say what BPHS Ch.94 actually
supplies: `BPHS Ch.94 (devatā attribution); mantra: nāma-mantra form (constructed)` —
so the citation stops doing work it cannot support.**

Basis: this is a product call layered on a factual one. The factual part (BPHS Ch.94
gives the devatā table, not mantra text; the nāma-mantra is constructed; TB 3.1.1–2 is
where per-nakshatra ṛks actually live) matches the classical record and the
investigator's findings. The product part: Madhav serves practitioners and seekers,
and a recitable nāma-mantra is the usable japa form; a TB ṛk with sandhi and Vedic
accent is scholarly gold but not what a remedy-seeking user recites from a phone
screen. Serve the usable form; attest it with the scholarly source; never imply the
citation supplies the mantra when it supplies the deity.

Confidence: HIGH on the citation correction (it is a pure honesty fix); product-call
on primacy of the nāma-mantra form — consistent with the owner's stated objective
(Acharya-grade *completeness and correctness*, which the attestation lane supplies)
rather than a change of product shape.
