## Key Tajaka-specific divergences from Parashari (for concordance layer)

1. **Aspect directionality:** Tajaka Itthasala/Ishrafa is direction-sensitive (applying vs. separating); Parashari aspects are direction-neutral. This is the single most important divergence for multi-school analysis.

2. **Moiety orbs vs. full-sign:** Tajaka uses precise degree-based moiety orbs (ranging from 3.5° for Mercury/Venus to 7.5° for Sun); Parashari aspects cast from sign to sign without orb.

3. **Parashari special aspects not operative in Tajaka:** Mars 4th/8th, Jupiter 5th/9th, Saturn 3rd/10th aspects are NOT used in annual chart analysis. Only the 5 standard Tajaka aspects (0°, 60°, 90°, 120°, 180°) apply.

4. **Annual-chart-only scope:** Tajaka has no natal equivalent; Muntha, Varshesh, Sahams, and Itthasala have no Parashari natal equivalents. The two systems operate at different temporal levels (lifetime vs. single year).

5. **Muntha as primary indicator:** Muntha (progressed annual ascendant, 1 sign/year) has no Parashari equivalent. It acts as the annual chart's environmental tone-setter alongside the Varshesh.

6. **Dasha-annual relationship:** Tajaka explicitly requires natal Dasha confirmation before annual chart predictions; the annual chart cannot create events the Dasha doesn't support. This is architecturally parallel to BPHS's Dasha-transit integration principle.

7. **No Jaimini Karakas in Tajaka:** Jaimini's Atmakaraka, Darakaraka, Putrakaraka system is not imported into annual chart analysis. Tajaka uses its own dignity system (Hayyiz, exaltation, kendra, lordship, aspects received).

8. **Sahams vs. Upagrahas:** Tajaka Sahams (Arabic Parts) are mathematically similar to Jaimini's Upapadha in concept (derived sensitive points) but the formulas and interpretive framework are entirely different. No direct mapping.

## Notes for orchestrator

Tajaka is a methodologically self-contained system for annual horoscopy only. Its most
important divergences from Parashari, Jaimini, and KP are: (1) direction-sensitive applying
aspects (Itthasala/Ishrafa) with precise moiety orbs; (2) Muntha and Varshesh as primary
annual indicators with no equivalent in other systems; (3) explicit Dasha-dependence (annual
chart cannot override the natal Dasha promise); (4) the five Tajaka yogas (Harsha, Sarala,
Vimala, Kamboola, Khallasara) are annual-chart-only configurations with no direct Parashari
equivalent. Rules covering these divergences are catalogued in ch1.v6, ch1.v7, ch1.v9, ch1.v10,
ch1.v12 and are designed for direct use in the concordance.yaml cross-school comparison layer.
